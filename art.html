Five principles

What the old strategy actually teaches a modern page

Sun Tzu wrote for generals. The lessons translate almost exactly to the six seconds a visitor gives a landing page before deciding to stay or go.

I

The art of focus

Sun Tzu's armies won by controlling what the enemy noticed and what they didn't. A landing page wins the same way — one goal, one path, one action, with every distraction quietly removed so attention flows toward the thing that matters.

From: all warfare is a matter of shaping perception
II

Speed over prolonged effort

No army benefits from a long campaign, and no visitor benefits from a slow page. Load fast, say the important thing first, and let a visitor arrive at a decision in seconds rather than scroll after scroll after scroll.

From: there is no instance of a state benefiting from a long war
III

Winning without a fight

The finest generalship broke resistance before the armies met. The finest landing pages do the same — they earn a "yes" through clarity and trust, not through countdown timers, guilt copy, or pop-ups the visitor has to fight past.

From: subduing the enemy without fighting is the height of skill
IV

Know your ground, know your visitor

Know the offer and know the visitor, and a hundred campaigns hold no danger. Analytics, heatmaps, and honest customer conversations are the modern scouts — the information that turns a guess into a page built on solid ground.

From: know yourself and know the other, and victory is never in doubt
V

Shaped like water

Water takes the shape of whatever ground it meets. A landing page should do the same — direct for a technical buyer, warm for a personal one, mobile-first where the audience already lives on a phone. One shape, built fresh for every market it meets.

From: an army has no fixed shape, as water has no fixed form
