What's actually happening

A shadow 290 kilometres wide

On August 12, 2026, the Moon will pass directly between the Earth and Sun, casting its darkest shadow — the umbra — onto the Atlantic and northern Spain just as the Sun nears the horizon.

Because the eclipse occurs only two days after lunar perigee, the Moon appears unusually large — wide enough to block the Sun along an exceptionally broad path, roughly 40% of mainland Spain.

It's mainland Europe's first total solar eclipse since 1999, and the first visible from the Iberian Peninsula since before living memory.

Before you look up

Watch it safely

!

Eye protection is non-negotiable

  • Use certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2) for every partial phase — sunglasses are never enough.
  • Only remove them during full totality — and only if you're inside the path of totality.
  • Outside the path (Madrid, Barcelona, etc.), the Sun is never fully covered — keep glasses on throughout.
  • Cameras and phones need a solar filter over the lens, or you risk damaging the sensor.
  • Find open western horizon — the eclipse happens low in the sky, close to sunset.
What comes after

The Iberian eclipse triad

2026 is the first of three consecutive solar eclipses visible from Spain — a run this rare hasn't happened in generations.

AUG 12, 2026

Total Solar Eclipse

Path of totality crosses Galicia, the north, and the Balearics at sunset. Madrid and Barcelona see a 99.9% partial eclipse.

AUG 2, 2027

Total Solar Eclipse

Visible again from Spain, plus North Africa and the Middle East — one of the longest totalities of the century.

JAN 26, 2028

Annular Solar Eclipse

A "ring of fire" eclipse visible from Spain, Portugal and South America, completing the triad.

2053

Next total eclipse from Spain

After 2028, Spain won't see another total solar eclipse for around 25 years.

Don't miss it

Two days from now, the sky over Spain goes dark in the middle of a summer evening. Get your eclipse glasses ready, find your spot facing west, and look up.
