HINGE MECHANICS / BOOK-STYLE FOLDABLES

Where do you put your thumb?

A folded phone is a closed book. Getting it open one-handed comes down to a few millimetres at the spine — whether there's anywhere for a fingertip to catch before you can start prying.

0.1–0.3mm The rough range separating a hinge edge you can hook a nail into from one you can't. It's a small margin, and manufacturers land on opposite sides of it on purpose.

Four hinges, one clear outlier

Most current book-style foldables can be opened one-handed with a bit of leverage — but "can" and "easily" are different claims. Here's where each one falls.

Two hands required

iPhone Duo

Apple (rumored)

Closes flush with no gap and no edge to hook a nail into. There's simply nowhere for a thumb to start the lever — so opening it means committing both hands every time.

Catch point: none — design failure, not a hinge trade-off
Easiest

HONOR Magic V5

HONOR

A deliberately widened gap at the spine when closed — a byproduct of the edge design, not an accident. That gap is exactly wide enough to get a fingernail in and start the lever.

Catch point: widest of the three
Easy

OPPO Find N5

OPPO

Less about the gap itself, more about everything around it — thin, light, and gripped securely enough that the hinge action feels smooth once it starts moving.

Catch point: moderate, compensated by grip
Harder

Galaxy Z Fold 7

Samsung

Sharper, flatter, and closed almost flush. There's no obvious place to hook a finger under — one comparison found this and the Magic V5 similarly awkward to grip at the edge.

Catch point: narrowest, least forgiving

So the criticism holds up

"I have to use two hands just to open the damn thing."

That's a legitimate usability complaint — not a design law of foldables. A book-style hinge doesn't have to demand both hands; HONOR and OPPO have both clearly designed for the moment your thumb goes looking for an edge to catch.

The iPhone Duo is the case that makes the complaint stick. Its flush, gapless closure leaves no edge for a thumb to catch, so opening it always takes two hands. That's not an unavoidable cost of the book format — it's a choice, and on this measure, the wrong one.

For a phone that's supposed to work on a whim — pulled out of a pocket, glanced at, opened without setting anything else down — one-handed opening isn't a nice-to-have. It's a detail worth judging a phone on, and it's exactly where the Duo falls down.
