Uroko (鱗) means "scales," referring to the triangular motif that echoes the pattern found on fish, snakes, and dragons in traditional Japanese design. Formed by tessellating triangles into a continuous zigzag, Uroko has long been used to ward off evil and disease, appearing on samurai armor, kimono, and noh theater costumes worn by serpent or demon characters. Its interlocking, protective structure has made it a lasting symbol of resilience and transformation.
